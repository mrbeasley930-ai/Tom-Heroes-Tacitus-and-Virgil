Two men the Romans called villains

Sejanus was the commander of the Praetorian Guard, the emperor Tiberius’ right-hand man, and for a few years the most powerful man in Rome after the emperor himself. He was executed in AD 31. Mezentius is an Etruscan king in Virgil’s Aeneid, so cruel that his own people rose up and burned his house. The Eduqas booklet heads the Tacitus passage “A Hero or a Villain?” and the Virgil “A King’s Gruesome Behaviour”. Your job today: translate both texts so that you own every word, then work out exactly how each writer makes you judge his man.

1 · Sejanus: the portrait — Annals 4.1

Tacitus opens Book 4 of the Annals by stopping the story to sketch the man who will dominate it. Read the whole passage aloud first, then copy the notes, then translate it sentence by sentence.

1nunc Seiani originem, mores expediam. genitus Vulsiniis patre
2equite Romano, et cum iuvenis esset divi Augusti nepotem
3sectatus est. mox Seianus Tiberium variis artibus devinxit adeo ut
4imperatorem obscurum adversum alios sibi uni incautum
5intectumque efficeret, non tam sollertia (quippe isdem artibus victus
6est) quam deorum ira in rem Romanam, cuius pari exitio viguit

2 · Sejanus: the cave — Annals 4.59

Some years later, AD 26. Tiberius is travelling in Campania with Sejanus beside him. This is the moment that seals the emperor’s trust.

12ac forte illis diebus Caesari anceps periculum oblatum est
13praebuitque ipsi materiem cur amicitiae constantiaeque Seiani
14magis fideret. vescebantur in villa, cui vocabulum Speluncae, mare
15Amunclanum inter et Fundanos montes nativo in specu. eius os
16lapsis repente saxis obruit quosdam ministros: hinc metus in omnes
17et fuga eorum qui convivium celebrabant. Seianus genu utroque et
18manibus super Caesarem suspensus opposuit sese incidentibus
19atque habitu tali repertus est a militibus qui subsidio venerant. maior
20ex eo et, quamquam exitiosa suaderet, ut non sui anxius cum fide
21audiebatur.
[today’s date] Sejanus 2 — the cave at Spelunca, AD 26
QuestionAnswer
Where?A villa called Spelunca (“the Cave”; modern Sperlonga), dining room inside a natural cave on the coast between Amunclae and the hills of Fundi
What happened?Rocks fell from the cave mouth and crushed some servants; the guests fled
What did Sejanus do?Arched himself over Tiberius on knees and hands, taking the falling rocks; found like that by the soldiers
ResultTiberius trusted him more than ever; his advice was heard cum fide
The stingquamquam exitiosa suaderet — even so, his advice was ruinous
A heroic act can be read two ways: what he did, and what Tacitus tells us it was for.

3 · Mezentius — Aeneid 8.478–495

Aeneas has sailed up the Tiber to Pallanteum, the little town on the future site of Rome, to ask its king Evander for help. Evander is speaking. He is about to explain why a whole Etruscan army is waiting for a foreign leader — and why that leader could be Aeneas.

478haud procul hinc saxo incolitur fundata vetusto
479urbis Agyllinae sedes, ubi Lydia quondam
480gens, bello praeclara, iugis insedit Etruscis.
481hanc multos florentem annos rex deinde superbo
482imperio et saevis tenuit Mezentius armis.
483quid memorem infandas caedes, quid facta tyranni
484effera? di capiti ipsius generique reservent!
485mortua quin etiam iungebat corpora vivis
486componens manibusque manus atque oribus ora,
487tormenti genus, et sanie taboque fluentes
488complexu in misero longa sic morte necabat.
489at fessi tandem cives infanda furentem
490armati circumsistunt ipsumque domumque,
491obtruncant socios, ignem ad fastigia iactant.
492ille inter caedem Rutulorum elapsus in agros
493confugere et Turni defendier hospitis armis.

495regem ad supplicium praesenti Marte reposcunt.
[today’s date] Mezentius — Aeneid 8: who is who
NameWho / what
Evanderthe speaker; Greek king of Pallanteum (site of Rome); Aeneas’ new ally
AgyllaEtruscan city (later Caere); “the Lydian race” = the Etruscans, believed to have come from Lydia in Asia Minor

Everything Mezentius is, Aeneas is not: saevus against pius.

5 · Style: how the writer does it

The 4-, 6- and 8-mark questions. The examiner wants close reference to the Latin and an explanation of effect. Copy the method and the technique table, then write four answers.

[today’s date] Answering a style question — the four-step move
1. Quote the Latin word or phrase.
2. Say what the writer has done with it (position, sound, choice of word, contrast, repetition).
3. Explain the effect on the reader — what you see, feel or think because of it.
4. Link it back to the question (hero? villain? vivid? shocking?).
TechniqueIn these passages
Antithesis / contrastpalam … intus; sui obtegens, in alios criminator; mortua … vivis
Asyndeton (no ‘and’)circumsistunt … obtruncant … iactant; the whole list in 4.1
Ellipsis (verb left out)corpus illi laborum tolerans, animus audax
Polyptoton (same word, different case)manibusque manus atque oribus ora
Word position / enjambmentlonga sic morte necabat; Mezentius ending its line
Metonymypraesenti Marte = with immediate war
Authorial commentquippe isdem artibus victus est; quamquam exitiosa suaderet
Latin word → what he did with it → effect → link. A technique named without its effect scores nothing.

6 · The verdict

The essay question sits behind the whole theme: what makes a hero, what makes a villain, and who decides? Copy the scales, then write two short judgements.

[today’s date] Hero or villain? — the scales
Looks heroicReads as villainous
Sejanuscourage and self-sacrifice in the cave; corpus laborum tolerans; industria ac vigilantiacompositus pudor, summa apiscendi libido; his virtues are feigned parando regno; his advice exitiosa; Tacitus calls him the gods’ anger against Rome
Mezentiusa king of a famous warrior people (bello praeclara)superbo imperio, saevis armis; infandas caedes; torture by binding the dead to the living; his own people rise in just fury; sheltered by Aeneas’ enemy
Who is telling us? Tacitus, writing with hindsight; Evander, who wants Aeneas to fight Mezentius.
“Villain” is a verdict. In the exam you must show the evidence in the Latin that leads to it.
